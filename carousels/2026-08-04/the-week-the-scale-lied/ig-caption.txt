why the scale goes up when you train

Three things happened that week, and the scale could only see one number.

Tuesday you trained hard. Muscle takes on water and glycogen to repair itself, and that water has weight. Friday you ate salt, and water follows salt — that is just how sodium works. Neither of those is fat.

But a bathroom scale measures total mass. It cannot tell muscle from water from fat, so it adds the lot together, hands you one figure, and leaves you to decide what it means. Most people decide it means they failed.

Weight and body composition are not the same measurement. A week that reads as a failure on the scale can be a good week in every way that actually matters.
